# Contributing to the Coaching Trust Framework

*French version: [CONTRIBUTING.fr.md](./CONTRIBUTING.fr.md).*

CTF is at an early stage: a vision and draft proposals, called RFCs ("requests for comments"). Nothing has been tested yet. This is the best moment to influence it: **comments are welcome now**, even before the formal comment periods open.

You do not need to be a developer, and you do not need a GitHub account.

## Ways to take part

| You are… | You can… |
|---|---|
| A coach or a supervisor | Tell us whether a requirement matches good coaching practice; review a test scenario in your language; point out anything unclear |
| A clinician | Check the expected behaviour in distress situations (RFC 0008, R3, R4, R7) |
| A researcher | Challenge the method (RFC 0010); propose a study using the research avenues in the [vision](./VISION.md#7-a-field-for-research) |
| A platform or agent publisher | Tell us whether the requirements are testable and fair; test your own agent once the tool is available |
| A federation or professional body | Discuss governance and the future consortium |
| Willing to review test conversations | Later, read simulated conversations and say whether the agent met each criterion (RFC 0010, section 11); tell us if you are interested |

## Four questions for coaches

You do not need to read everything. Read the requirements of [RFC 0008](./rfcs/0008-standard-v0-starting-requirements.md) (sections R1 to R8), then answer any of these questions in a few lines:

1. Does each expected behaviour match good coaching practice and ethics as you know them?
2. Is there a behaviour counted as a failure that you would accept from a human coach, or the reverse?
3. Is anything missing that you would consider essential for an AI coaching agent?
4. Which sentences did you not understand?

## How to comment

1. Read the proposal you want to comment on. The list is in [rfcs/README.md](./rfcs/README.md). Each RFC exists in English and in French.
2. Send your comment by one of these means:
   - **with a GitHub account:** open an [issue](https://github.com/macsimplex/coaching-trust-framework/issues) whose title starts with the RFC number, for example "RFC 0008 — R7: ...";
   - **without a GitHub account:** write to maxime@simplex.coach, with the RFC number in the subject.
3. Comments may be written in English or in French; comments on the French texts count as much as comments on the English ones, even though the English version is authoritative in case of divergence. Say whether you agree to your name and organisation being shown next to your comment; otherwise it is shown anonymously.

Every comment received during a comment period is copied into the "Public comments" section of the RFC, with its date.

## How to bring evidence

Evidence is what makes a proposal change: an observation, a study, a test result, an experience from practice. Describe what you observed, how, and its limits. Send it the same way as a comment, with "Evidence" in the title or subject.

## Who decides, for now

Until a consortium exists, **the author of CTF, Maxime Bui, decides**. In return, he commits to:

- answering every comment received during a comment period;
- explaining every decision in writing, in the "Caretaker decision" section of the RFC (the caretaker is the author, until a consortium exists), including why comments were or were not followed;
- never changing a published version of the standard: any change produces a new version;
- handing decisions over to a consortium once it is formed, under rules that will themselves be proposed as an RFC.

## Comment periods

A comment period lasts 30 days from the date an RFC is opened. RFCs 0001 to 0005 were restored from an earlier version of the repository; their comment period is paused and will restart when they are reviewed again. RFCs 0008 to 0010 are drafts; their formal comment period has not opened yet, but comments sent now are welcome and will be taken into account.

## Contributing to the text directly

If you are comfortable with GitHub, you may propose a change to a text through a pull request. Keep each pull request to one subject, and say which RFC it concerns.
